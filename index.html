Project concept · v2.0 · for field & technical partners

Rapid-deployment, climate-adapted classrooms for children in Cox's Bazar, Bangladesh.

In the Rohingya refugee camps of Cox's Bazar — the world's largest refugee settlement — hundreds of thousands of children are out of school, and the bamboo learning centres that do exist are exposed to cyclones, monsoon floods, fire and extreme heat. Cuick School proposes a standardized modular classroom unit — a retrofitted shipping container transported and assembled quickly in low-resource environments, designed for the region's climate hazards, and paired with established emergency-education curricula.

The problem

In the world's largest refugee settlement, about 350,000 children are out of school.

More than half of the camps' one million Rohingya refugees are children. Classes meet in small bamboo shelters, often without desks, chairs or power — and by early 2026, aid cuts had closed about half of them.

Why shipping containers?

R1

A global surplus

Used ISO containers are abundant and inexpensive to source in port economies like South Korea — turning a waste stream into school infrastructure.

R2

Transport-native

A container moves through the world's shipping network as standard cargo — Korea to Chattogram via a regional hub and a feeder ship, no special freight. The school is its own crate.

R3

Fire- and storm-resistant

Unlike bamboo and plastic sheeting, a steel shell doesn't burn and can be anchored against cyclone winds. It also heats up fast — which is why passive cooling comes first in our design.

Built to humanitarian standards — and to local rules.

The project operates under a written child safeguarding commitment. We develop the model against the frameworks the sector already uses and inside the rules that govern the camps — and we actively seek partner review of our climate and safeguarding approach to ensure full compliance before any deployment, not after.

Education in emergencies
INEE Minimum Standards
The unit and its programming are developed in reference to the INEE Minimum Standards for Education in Emergencies.
Shelter & WASH
Sphere Standards
Shelter and water, sanitation, and hygiene considerations are guided by the Sphere Standards.
Bangladesh camp rules
Camp authorities & the Education Sector
Permanent school buildings aren't allowed in the camps, so the unit is being designed to be temporary and fully removable. Nothing deploys without approval from camp authorities (RRRC and Camp-in-Charge) and coordination with the Education Sector, co-led by UNICEF and Save the Children.

The long-term vision

From one unit to a campus.

The first container is proof, not the finish line. Cuick School is designed from day one as a system that scales — structurally, energetically, and locally.

V1

Modular, Lego-style scaling

The unit is the building block, not the building. Standardized units combine into multi-classroom clusters and, eventually, full campuses — with layouts adapted to each region's climate, terrain, and community needs.

V2

Smart energy systems Roadmap

Solar generation paired with battery storage and simple load management, so a unit banks power where electricity access is inconsistent — keeping lights, fans, and lesson screens running through the school day.

V3

Locally run, by design

Deployment ends with handover, not dependence. Local teams assemble and maintain units, Rohingya and host-community teachers deliver the lessons, and partner NGOs anchor operations on the ground.

Our solution

The unit, the program, and the journey between.

A modular classroom unit built on four principles — fast to stand up, safe in the heat, ready to teach from day one, and cheap enough to repeat.

01

Rapid deployment

Pre-fabricated, flat-packable components assembled on-site by a small trained team with minimal equipment — days of assembly instead of months of construction.

02

Climate adaptation

Passive ventilation, a reflective and insulated envelope, and shading to keep interiors in a safe learning range — on a raised, anchored, non-combustible shell designed for a coast hit by cyclones, monsoon floods and camp fires.

03

Curriculum-ready

Each unit pairs with proven emergency-education (EiE) kits and the Myanmar-curriculum programs already taught in the camps, rather than reinventing pedagogy — managed by our Textbook Team below.

04

Low-cost & repeatable

A standardized bill of materials enabling predictable per-unit cost and scaling across sites — the same spec, every time, anywhere.

From Songdo to the camps.

The model's quiet advantage: the classroom ships as its own crate. Brighter Boxes sources a surplus shipping container in Korea, retrofits it to the Cuick spec, packs it with its own education payload, and sends it through the same logistics network as any commercial cargo — ending with about 150 km by road from Chattogram port to the camps at Ukhia. The Textbook Team — remote curriculum management.

A container is only a classroom if there's something worth teaching inside it. Our education team (Pillar A) builds and manages that layer remotely from Songdo — designed for settings with low literacy, no internet, and limited electricity.

A1

Adapt, don't reinvent

We package proven emergency-education materials aligned with the Myanmar curriculum used in the camps' learning centres — taught in Burmese and English, with Rohingya-language support — rather than writing pedagogy from scratch.

A2

Offline by design

Printed subject packets in math, reading, health, and art, plus short video micro-lessons for TV/USB playback. No internet connection required, ever.

A3

School-in-a-Box

Every unit ships with a complete teacher kit — lesson guides, materials, and supplies — so a community teacher can run a functional classroom from day one.

A4

Managed remotely, refreshed physically

Curriculum is versioned in Songdo and updated through USB refresh packs and reprints delivered on partner visits — with a feedback loop from local teachers shaping every revision.

What we're asking of partners

Three things, in order of priority.

We're looking for the experience and judgment of partners already working in Cox's Bazar — in education, shelter, and logistics. Concretely, we're asking for:

01

A short conversation

To align our model with field realities and the priorities of the Cox's Bazar Education Sector.

02

A technical review

Of our climate, fire-safety and safeguarding approach, by your specialists.

03

Guidance toward a demonstration unit

Toward a possible supervised demonstration unit at an appropriate site — in the camps or the surrounding host communities, with the authorities' approval.
